# 20 October 2026: original-data audit, additional observations and colour identifiability

**Project initiator and maintainer: Shota Moriwaki. ChatGPT-assisted analysis and writing; unreviewed research record.** [日本語](FOLLOWUP_2026-10-20.ja.md) · [Research overview](README.md) · [Previous follow-up](FOLLOWUP_2026-10-19.en.md)

This round completed an original-SDSS audit of every previously unresolved MWDD SDSS representation, actual individual viewing of all 613 newly resolved records, native-pixel support checks, additional imaging photometry, repeated-spectrum shape analysis, and optimization over whole opposite-colour sets.

The strongest methodological result is a concrete limitation: **under an explicitly allowed wavelength-dependent calibration family, spectra fitting the observed broadband measurements can retain opposite colour orderings even with numerically identical predictions in 14 proposed bands.** Actual additional photometry constrains this freedom but does not make the principal near-boundary result insensitive to the model assumptions. This is a reproducible, object-specific identifiability result, not a new atmosphere or opacity measurement.

## 2. Units and observed support were corrected separately

Official SDSS queries found possible observations within 30 arcsec for 1,568 of the 1,579 unresolved exports; 11 had no retrieved candidate reference. Position alone did not establish representation identity. Every tested pair had to meet the strict wavelength/pixel and transformation residual requirements.

The new identifications comprise **178 Fν-shaped and 435 Fλ-shaped exports**. With previous results, the 2,232 MWDD SDSS records now contain **327 Fν / 939 Fλ / 966 unresolved** cases. Up to a constant scale, converting Fν to Fλ changes the shape by λ⁻². The registry never decides units from continuum slope alone. [All pair decisions](unit_resolution_followup/unit_evidence_all.csv) · [Explicit overlay](unit_resolution_followup/overlay_registry.json)

Visual inspection then exposed long straight segments not supported by valid original measurements. In row 8295, for example, 704 of 3,620 visible native SDSS pixels have zero IVAR; the largest gap between positive-IVAR pixels is 1,113 Å. The MWDD export omitted error information, allowing that region to appear continuously measured.

All 613 newly resolved records were therefore recalculated using finite positive original IVAR, a finite MWDD-export counterpart within 0.02 Å, and the previously fixed narrow sky windows. Existing calibration coefficients were held fixed; no new fit concealed the effect of changing support. Blanket removal of all AND/OR flags was not adopted. An AND-zero sensitivity remains distinct.

Ten records previously accepted by the compatibility recipe now have their scientific colours withheld. Two newly unit-resolved records were already ineligible. **Representation identity does not imply adequate wavelength support, absolute calibration, source association or physical classification.** [Native support audit](unit_resolution_followup/native_quality_summary.json)

### J1323−0129: what changed

A new original reference, `spec-4049-55591-0832.fits`, provides 3,507 matched samples and establishes the MWDD SDSS representation as **Fλ**. Qualitative agreement in positive Duv with DESI can therefore be recovered.

Precision independent corroboration remains withheld: the native 4000–7500 Å median pixel S/N is about 2.36, and the epoch-propagated Gaia position is about 1.14 arcsec from the original fibre position. Across the raw, existing-gri and PS1-sensitivity states, the SDSS/DESI separation remains approximately 0.0045–0.0054 in uv; the existing-gri state gives approximately 0.00494. These are not the same calibration definitions as the standardized headline DESI value Duv = +0.023498, which is not overwritten. Native Gaia XP remains unavailable. ## 4. New databases supplied actual measurements and prior classifications

SkyMapper DR4, J-PLUS DR4, Gaia and the published J-PLUS CDS catalogue were queried for the frozen candidates. Exact Gaia IDs were combined with proper-motion-propagated observing-epoch positions. Several nearby bright false matches were rejected; for J1323, even a different imaging source carrying the same Gaia cross-reference was rejected in favour of the correct positional counterpart.

The acquisition checked 110 individual SkyMapper detections. Original J-PLUS Fν photometry independently reproduces all 84 corresponding AB magnitudes and errors to catalogue rounding precision. Already-applied zero points are not added twice, and FWHM pixel/arcsec definitions remain separate. All 450 slots, rejected counterparts, flags, epochs and aperture diagnostics are preserved. [Additional-photometry report](independent_photometry/README.md)

SkyMapper and J-PLUS are different photon observations, but their zero points share Gaia calibration, and J-PLUS uses a white-dwarf locus and PS1 r anchor. The errors cannot simply be multiplied as independent probabilities. Public S-PLUS mirror queries were also attempted with alternative column syntax, but coordinate-column resolution failed. The 25 failures remain errors, not nondetections or flux upper limits.

### Actual impact on J0429

At 800 Å spline spacing, the minimum maximum shape change needed to fit PS1 griz increases from about **4.25% to 4.92%** after adding strict SkyMapper r/i. Strict green requires about **5.01%**, and strict R > G about **4.924%**. Adding 0.02 mag to the extra error-box half-widths returns both minima to approximately 4.25%. These are sensitivity assumptions, not measured calibration-error distributions, and the near-5% distinction is not a robust colour determination.

The same calculation was applied to every measured target. The large J1031 i residual and smooth-family incompatibilities for J0716/J0006 were retained. Exact source joins show that J0006 is already a published metal-outlier case in [López-Sanjuan et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202555286); J1252 belongs to that paper's random-fluctuation outlier group and J1841 is not an outlier under its test. Catalogue release changes and prior classifications are not new independent discoveries.

## 5. Whole-set filter design produced a concrete counterexample

Instead of merely comparing two previously saved spectra, the experiment lets every allowed green-side and opposite-side spectrum vary independently. It minimizes the largest normalized difference in their proposed band fluxes, while requiring both to fit the same actual PS1 griz boxes and continuous shape bound.

For J0429 at a 5% bound, 800 Å spline spacing yields a minimum ten-J-PLUS-band separation of about **0.2593% of baseline flux**. The dual lower bound gives a uniform hard-box half-width sufficient condition of about **0.1296%**. However, at 400 Å spacing, a strict opposite-colour pair has numerically identical predictions in all **14 PS1 + covered J-PLUS bands**. No actual J-PLUS measurement of J0429 was acquired.

![Constructed opposite-colour witnesses with numerically identical 14-band predictions](measurement_design/indistinguishable_fourteen_band_witnesses.png)

The implication is specific: empirical constraints on the wavelength scale of calibration freedom matter alongside the number of filters. The witnesses are constructed on one noisy observed baseline; they are not two observed states or self-consistent atmosphere models. A zero distance establishes an ambiguous common measurement vector, not ambiguity for every possible future measurement. General metamerism is prior knowledge. [Full equations, results and independent verification](measurement_design/README.md)

## 6. Actual repeated observations quantify shape differences, with limits

The complete DESI pairs use common native support, fixed masks, a grey 6500–7100 Å anchor and covariance induced by that normalization. Archive g−r differences are +0.0286 mag for J0111, −0.0248 for J1105 and +0.0602 for J1252. J1329's extra exposure has an entirely masked Z arm, so complete colour and i/z are withheld.

The SDSS case, J1410−0235 / Gaia `3646912496889350784`, is **two nonoverlapping coadds built from five nights and 18 physical exposures**, not merely two nights. Original headers, fibre positions and exact photometry IDs were checked. Its g−r difference changes from about 0.3127 mag to 0.0620 after separate gri fits; unused z still differs by 0.1196 mag. The 800 Å descriptive shape RMS changes from 26.24% to 11.18%, without demonstrating agreement outside the fitted anchors.

Original exposure seeing/airmass differ, but their role in the shape change was not quantitatively established. This selected pair is not a population calibration distribution. Weak blue flux makes the J1105 multiplicative description unstable. J0429 and J0913 each have one available exposure in the inspected inventory, so their own repeated-shape distributions were not measured. [Repeat-shape analysis](repeat_shape_validation/README.md)

## 7. Falsifiable research directions grounded in the original papers

[Hollands et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18966) connect unusual white-dwarf colours to broadened atomic/molecular opacity in dense atmospheres. The present audit makes the next tests more precise:

| Question or hypothesis | Evidence obtained here | Decisive test and falsification condition |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration wavelength dependence controls near-boundary identifiability. | J0429 changes across spline scales and added-photometry boxes. | Freeze a calibration covariance model using comparable standards/repeats before candidate testing; predict whether colour sets separate under it. |
| Free Na/Ca or Mg/Ca improves predictions beyond fixed metal ratios. | Existing DZ models use restrictive abundance assumptions; broad-band agreement can coexist with local residuals. | Compare self-consistent atmosphere models under equal calibration freedom using unused bands and line wings. Reject abundance claims if the improvement disappears when calibration is allowed. |
| Some DQ discrepancies reflect the single-atmosphere assumption. | Extra bands expose inconsistent shapes; H/He controls do not explain carbon structure. | Compare carbon, pressure-shift, magnetic and two-component alternatives against Gaia-constrained absolute SEDs and unused measurements. A successful updated single atmosphere removes the need for a binary explanation. |
| Epoch changes and calibration changes can be separated by line/continuum predictions. | Repeated spectra retain shape and unused-band differences. | Simultaneous photometry, repeated line profiles and comparable controls; changes also seen in controls weaken intrinsic-variability attribution. |
| Exported errors and support can generate apparent colour outliers. | Ten new native-support failures persist after correct unit identification. | Keep unit, support and calibration operations separate in original-pixel regression checks; advance only unexplained residuals to physical modelling. |
| Mixed source classes affect photospheric interpretation. | A known AM CVn and additional catalogue/quasar conflicts occur in individually flagged cases. | Retain confirmed single-star, known-binary and uncertain-class context; cross-check original catalogues and independent evidence before atmosphere fits. |
| Population occurrence needs the selection function. | Archive coverage, duplicates and targeted selection are heterogeneous. | Predefined population, injection/recovery through measurement and selection, matched controls and multiplicity accounting; do not use 419 selected records as a discovery-rate numerator. |
| Useful observations can be designed from whole-set separation. | Full opposite-colour-set distances are computable for real responses. | Include empirically justified calibration and response errors, freeze the design and test its predicted ambiguity or separation with new observations. |

A new physical result still requires native self-consistent composition-specific atmosphere arrays and an empirically justified calibration model. The previous public H/He tables and published DZ/DQ figures are useful controls/diagnostics, but plotted curves do not support new abundance likelihoods. The new measurements provide concrete predictions to test when those model inputs become available.

The presently defensible contributions are a reproducible original-observation representation/support audit and object-specific colour-identifiability bounds constrained by actual photometry. Specialist novelty and validity review remains necessary; no worldwide-priority or new-physics claim is made.
